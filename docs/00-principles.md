# 00 · Design Principles

*Layer: Core (stable). Changes to this document require a MAJOR version.*

These ten principles constrain every other part of HACF. When an indicator, task or tool conflicts with a principle, the principle wins.

### P1 — Capability, not familiarity
HACF measures what a person can **achieve** with AI, not which products they have heard of. Tool knowledge is evidence only when it produces outcomes.

### P2 — Vendor and technology neutrality
No level, dimension or descriptor names a specific vendor, model or product. Products appear only in non-normative examples, which are expected to go out of date.

### P3 — Relationship, not technique
Levels are defined by the person's **relationship to AI** — observing, asking, collaborating, delegating, orchestrating, governing. These relationships remain meaningful as AI systems move from chat assistants to agents to autonomous systems; specific techniques (e.g. "prompting") do not.

### P4 — Judgment and responsibility are not optional
A person who produces a lot with AI but cannot verify it or handle data correctly is a risk, not an expert. Judgment (J) and Responsibility (R) gate every level from L1 upward.

### P5 — Level is not position
Levels are independent of job title, seniority, education or department. A target level for a role is a business decision; the measured level is a fact about the person.

### P6 — Multidimensional by default
A single number hides the most useful information. Every result is reported as a level **and** a five-dimension profile.

### P7 — Evidence over self-report
Self-assessment is useful for engagement but weak as proof. Higher levels require stronger evidence: practical tasks, real work artefacts, sustained outcomes.

### P8 — Proportionality
Required capability depends on context: the risk of the AI use, the autonomy of the system and the consequences of error. A call-centre agent supervising an autonomous system may need higher J than a marketer drafting posts.

### P9 — Development, not surveillance
HACF exists to develop people and make better organisational decisions. Results must not be used as a sole basis for dismissal, and individual data must be handled under applicable data-protection law and with informed participation.

### P10 — Open and evolvable
The framework is openly licensed, versioned, and changed through a public, documented process. Empirical evidence from use outranks the author's opinion.
