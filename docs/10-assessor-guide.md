# 10 · Assessor Guide

*Layer: Indicators.*

## Who can assess

An assessor is assessed at **L3 or higher with J≥3** and has completed a calibration round (below). For L4/L5 decisions, at least one assessor is L4+ or L5 respectively.

## Calibration (before first use, then yearly)

1. Each assessor independently scores the same **10 anonymised submissions** of a practical task.
2. Compare scores dimension by dimension. Target: **exact agreement ≥ 60%, within ±1 point ≥ 90%**.
3. Discuss every 2-point disagreement and write down the decision as an **anchor example** (one paragraph: what was observed, which score, why).
4. Keep anchors in a private calibration file; they become the organisation's scoring memory.

## Scoring discipline

- Score what is **observed in the submission and log**, not reputation, title or confidence.
- A score needs at least **one concrete observation** written in the report.
- If a planted defect was missed, J cannot exceed 2 for that task, whatever else was done well.
- If personal data was sent to an unapproved tool, R cannot exceed 1 for that task.
- If an embedded malicious instruction was followed, J and R cannot exceed 1.
- When unsure between two scores, give the **lower** one and note why.
- The tool used is irrelevant. A good result with a simple tool scores the same as with an advanced one.

## Interview prompts for artefact review (E4)

- What did you check, and how did you know it was right?
- When did it fail? What changed afterwards?
- Who can switch it off, and who is accountable for its output?
- What did you decide **not** to automate, and why?
- Show me a log, a test set or a decision record.

## Conflicts of interest

Do not assess your own direct reports for decisions that affect pay or promotion. Declare any personal relationship with the participant.

## Feedback to participants

Give individual feedback privately, starting with strengths, then up to three development priorities with concrete actions. Never compare a participant with named colleagues.
